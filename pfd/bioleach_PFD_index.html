Acid budget (2026-09-12, resolved 2026-09-13). Dissolved metals need ≈ 25 keq/h of counter-anions; the INL pulp-scaled basis (300 mM gluconate at 10 %) supplied ≈ 6 keq/h, a 4× deficit that PHREEQC [16] confirmed independently (cation excess 1.7 eq/L). Option A closes it with gluconate alone: 896 mM at 4.2 % pulp (15 % free-acid margin), charge ratio 1.13. Downstream base demand is now computed as what it takes to hold each unit's pH set-point in the ~1 M gluconate buffer (Fe/Al 5.5, Mn SX 4.2, Co SX 5.5, Ni SX 6.5), not one NaOH per gluconate — the leach already deprotonated the gluconate that balances the metals. Consequences carried through the flowsheet: R-202 575 m³ per vessel, glucose 4.8 t/h, NaOH 0.84 t/h, and a barren liquor that is ~240 g/L sodium gluconate — which is why lithium is taken as Li₃PO₄ before, not after, the evaporator (notes 28–29). Notes — circled numbers on the drawing

Recycle fraction is a design variable; a bleed on the raffinate loop (Area 500) controls sulfate and Na/Ca build-up — without it the loop is not closed, only slowly dying.
  3. Pulp-density control. A density transmitter on the P-101 discharge (DIC) trims the S-104 valve. This is the loop that guards the 7 % basis: solids are fixed by W-101, so water is the only free variable.

Design choice

  • Slurry in water, dose biolixiviant at the reactor. Keeps T-101 out of the Fe(II)/gluconate chemistry and lets pulp density and lixiviant dose be controlled independently. The alternative — slurrying straight into gluconate broth — saves nothing but the water line and couples two controls that should stay separate.

Notes — circled numbers on the drawing

  1. Seed train. Lab shake flask (off-sheet) → R-201A 50 L → R-201B 5 m³ → R-202 50 m³, 10 % inoculum at each step. Same shape as a pharma cell-culture train, at a fraction of the cost: the product is a dilute acid, not an injectable, so the plant is aseptic rather than sterile-critical, and gluconic acid at pH ~3 self-selects against most contaminants. Brewery-grade stainless with SIP/CIP, not GMP.
  2. Staggered batch, not continuous. (1) Product inhibition — G. oxydans stalls as gluconate accumulates and pH falls; a continuous fermenter runs at the outlet condition and can only hold low titer, which would multiply the broth volume dosed at R-301 and break the pulp-density basis. (2) Containment — a contaminated batch costs 24 h of one vessel; a contaminated continuous fermenter starves the leach. (3) Precedent — industrial gluconic acid has been batch/fed-batch since the 1950s and INL's demonstration was batch; no continuous reference plant exists. (4) The leach still sees continuous feed: A/B offset 12 h plus T-202's 12 h hold. (5) At the original 75 mM basis this was 60 m³, brewery scale; Option A (charge-balanced gluconate, 150 g/L broth) makes it ~960 m³ per vessel — large industrial fermenter scale (citric/gluconic plants run 100–300 m³ for real citric/gluconic plants), where the batch-vs-continuous case should be revisited.
  3. One sterile-air train. K-201 + F-202 0.2 µm feed all four vessels from one header. O₂ demand is ½ mol per glucose — ~410 kg/h, and the 0.5 vvm rate on the ~800 m³ working volume (~24,000 m³/h air) supplies several times the stoichiometric O₂; mass transfer, not stoichiometry, still sets the rate.
  4. Condensate return, not raffinate (revised at Sheet 5). The SX raffinate leaves Area 500 as a sodium-gluconate brine (~640 kg/h Na, ~5.0 t/h gluconate) that the fermenter cannot take, so the water that comes back is the evaporator and crystalliser condensate (S-517, ~22 t/h, clean). It supplies the medium water here and the surplus (S-204) goes to T-101 make-up on Sheet 1. Nutrients and gluconate therefore do not recycle — they leave in the brine bleed (S-516) to Sheet 6. Sheet 6 now closes the balance: condensate ~10.4 t/h covers the S-104 make-up and most of the medium, leaving ~6.9 t/h net fresh water. Condensing the D-601 exhaust (~7.8 t/h) would close it entirely (note ㉜).
  5. Broth water counts toward pulp density. On the Option A basis S-212 brings ~28 t/h of water into R-301 (33 m³/h of 150 g/L broth), so S-104 make-up water on Sheet 1 is only ~1,700 kg/h against a 16.6 t/h liquor; T-101 is now a small make-up tank rather than the ~43 % solids paste the 7 % basis required and the 4.2 % basis is reached in the reactor. Most of the leach water is broth — the fermenter is the water plant.

Notes — circled numbers on the drawing

  1. Batch leach, staggered. INL's 30 h / 55 °C / 86 % Co figure was measured in a batch vessel; a batch tank reproduces that condition exactly, where a CSTR train only approximates it through a residence-time distribution. Four tanks offset by 12.5 h give the continuous filter a steady feed via T-303. Same argument as the fermenters (note ⑨): batch where the kinetics were measured, continuous where the equipment wants it.
  2. Iron enters dry. ~352 kg/h FeSO₄·7H₂O — 4.1 kg per kg Co recovered (INL 3.75–4.51). At 28 % of the black-mass feed rate it is the single largest reagent, 10 % of OpEx, and every kilogram leaves as Fe(III) sludge on Sheet 4. Dosed as dry salt into the fill line so no water is added; a 20 % solution would have cut S-104 by another 1.4 t/h.
  3. Off-gas. Carbonate in the black mass (Li₂CO₃, SEI) releases CO₂ on acid contact; residual LiPF₆ can release HF. A small caustic scrubber is drawn rather than an open vent because of the HF, not the CO₂. CO₂ is a placeholder (30 % of Li as carbonate).
  4. Residue is the largest single loss. ~830 kg/h dry: graphite and binder plus the unleached 14 % of the Co (14.1 kg/h) and 16 % of the Ni. To Sheet 5 as waste for this flowsheet; graphite recovery and a second-stage residue leach are the two obvious upgrades, both needing data INL does not give.
  5. Wash filtrate closes the first water loop. S-312 (~1.6 t/h) is the "Sh 3" arrow on Sheet 1 — about 90 % of the S-104 make-up (make-up is small because the broth is most of the water). It carries the PLS displaced from the cake, so a few kg/h of dissolved Co/Ni cycle back through the leach at steady state. Not a loss, but the balance here is single-pass; steady-state build-up is a later refinement.
  6. Cu and Al leach fractions — literature-anchored, not measured. INL does not report them. Gluconic acid on its own barely dissolves copper (0.3 % Cu, 9.7 % Fe, 51.9 % Al — Lerchbammer et al. 2025 [8], an H₂O₂ system), but once Fe³⁺ is present metallic Cu in black mass is consumed as a reductant (Porvali et al. 2020 [19]; Partinen et al. 2024), and organic-acid bioleaching reaches 100 % Cu with 65–75 % Al (Horeh et al. 2016 [4]; Bahaloo-Horeh et al. 2017 [5]). Basis: Cu 80 %, Al 60 %; sensitivity Cu 0.3–100 %. Upside not taken: Cu's electrons would offset ~half the FeSO₄ dose, but INL's 4.1 kg/kg ratio was measured on Cu-bearing black mass, so the credit is assumed already inside it. Both fractions drive Sheet 4 (Cu poisons Co/Ni recovery; Al makes gelatinous hydroxide). Thermodynamic ceiling from thermo_speciation.py: see note ⑲.
  7. Thermodynamics of the PLS (PHREEQC, minteq.v4 + gluconate; re-run on Option A, 2026-09-13). Liquor from the balance (Co 4.0, Ni 8.7, Mn 2.9, Li 2.0, Cu 0.9, Al 1.0, Fe 3.3 g/L; gluconate 200 g/L ≈ 1.0 M; 55 °C, Fe as Fe(III)). (0) Charge balance is pH-dependent. Gluconic acid has pKa 3.86, so at the INL leach pH of 2.5 only a few % of the 1 M gluconate is the anion and PHREEQC still reports a 1.0 eq/L cation excess. The balanced liquor therefore cannot sit at pH 2.5: as metals dissolve and consume protons the pH rises to ~4.5–5, where gluconate is deprotonated and the budget closes. Leach pH is now a design variable, not the INL number. (a) Iron and aluminium drop out in the leach itself. With 1 M gluconate goethite is complete from pH 3 (Fe dissolved 0.2 %) and gibbsite takes Al from pH 4 (55 % out at 4.0, 91 % at 4.5, 99 % at 5.5); Co, Ni, Cu, Mn stay 100 % dissolved through pH 8, held by gluconate chelation. So on Option A most of the Fe/Al removal happens in R-301 and reports to the leach residue (S-308), and T-402's duty shrinks to a polish — but the Fe(III) oxidant is largely solid above pH 3 (only 3–45 % Fe(III)-gluconate in solution), which questions the FeSO₄ reductant chemistry at that pH. (b) Cu now stays dissolved to pH 8 (was 5 % on the 300 mM basis): cementation (T-401) remains the right removal step. (c) Fe(III)–gluconate log K still controls whether Fe is held in solution: ≤ 8 → Fe precipitates, 10 → 100 % dissolved at pH 3.5. Still the single most valuable lab measurement. Caveats: I ≈ 1 M is beyond the Davies model; 25 °C gluconate constants; goethite as control phase; equilibrium only. Output: thermo_results.txt.

Notes — circled numbers on the drawing

  1. Cementation puts iron back in. Every mole of Cu removed adds a mole of Fe²⁺ (~17 kg/h) to a liquor that is about to be de-ironed, and the excess powder (30 %) reports to the cement cake. Cost: an air-oxidation duty in T-402 and ~20 % more goethite. Benefit: no H₂S handling, no SX circuit for 20 kg/h of copper, and the cement is salable. The alternative sulfide route (NaHS at pH 2–3) would avoid the iron but needs a scrubbed vent.
  2. NaOH, not lime, for the Fe/Al step. Dose is set by the pH target in the gluconate buffer: after the leach the liquor already sits near pH 4.8, so reaching 5.5 while dropping Fe³⁺ and Al³⁺ as hydroxides takes ~390 kg/h NaOH (not the ~1.2 t/h a one-NaOH-per-gluconate count gives). Mn SX at pH 4.2 needs none; Co and Ni SX take ~190 and ~260 kg/h to hold 5.5 and 6.5 as the extractants release protons. This is a gluconate liquor with only ~6 g/L sulfate (from FeSO₄), so lime would strip all of it as gypsum and leave ~90 kg/h of dissolved Ca — which D2EHPA extracts ahead of Mn and which would contaminate every downstream product. Sodium goes to the raffinate bleed instead. Lime is kept for the effluent sheet, where gypsum is wanted.
  3. Co/Ni occlusion in the Fe/Al cake — placeholder 2 % each. Goethite/gibbsite co-precipitation loss is real but unmeasured here; 1.7 kg/h Co is ~$1.5 M/yr. The number to test first in the lab, together with cake filterability.
  4. D2EHPA runs at pH 3.0 and doubles as a scavenger. Extraction order Fe³⁺ > Al³⁺ > Ca > Mn > Co > Ni: whatever Fe/Al passes F-402 is taken here and leaves with the strip liquor. Co co-extraction is held to ~2 % by keeping pH ≤ 3; above ~3.5 cobalt follows manganese. Two extraction stages for 98 % Mn; scrub stage returns entrained Co/Ni.
  5. MnSO₄ is a by-product, not a headline. ~60 kg/h Mn as ~100 g/L strip liquor. Sold as liquor to a Mn refiner or crystallised later; not in the revenue basis. Carries ~1.6 kg/h Co that the scrub stage should reduce.

Notes — circled numbers on the drawing

  1. Cobalt first, Cyanex 272. The industry Co/Ni separation. Tait [12] measures pH₅₀ ≈ 5.3 for Co and ≈ 7.0 for Ni, so at pH 5.5–6.0 cobalt loads and nickel mostly stays. The window is real but narrower than the 4.5 / 6.5 pair often quoted in reviews. Saponified organic (NaOH pre-neutralised) so pH does not crash on loading; the sodium that adds joins the barren brine. Three extraction stages for 99 %, two scrub stages to hold Ni in the product below spec. Gluconate in the aqueous is a weak Co ligand (log K ~1.9) and does not compete with the extractant.
  2. Nickel by Versatic 10, not a second Cyanex circuit. Cyanex 272 takes Ni only near pH 7 where hydroxide risk starts; Versatic 10 at pH 6.5–7 is the standard Ni extractant over Li/Na/Ca. Strip with H₂SO₄ to a NiSO₄ liquor for crystallisation — the sulfate matrix for the product is made here, not inherited from the leach (this is a gluconate plant).
  3. Products are the hydrated sulfates. CoSO₄·7H₂O and NiSO₄·6H₂O at 86 % per-pass crystalliser yield [14], with the mother liquor recycled to the crystalliser feed and only a 5 % bleed leaving as S-518/519 to Sheet 6. Because the bleed rather than the per-pass yield sets the loss, overall crystalliser recovery is 99.2 %. Overall recovery to saleable product: Co 80 %, Ni 78 %, Li 94 % — the "downstream losses applied" answer to the 86 / 84 / 100 % leach figures. Lithium is now Li₃PO₄ (Keshav, 2026-09-13), not Li₂CO₃: 1,821 t/yr at 94 % Li recovery. Products 2,981 / 6,152 / 1,821 t/yr against the TEA basis of 3,265 / 6,285 / 1,164 (Li₂CO₃) — the TEA needs re-pricing on phosphate.
  4. The evaporator recovers water, not lithium. On Option A the barren liquor (S-512, ~23 t/h) is already ~240 g/L sodium gluconate, so it can never be concentrated 13× to a carbonate-grade 25 g/L Li — the salt would pass its ~590 g/L solubility long before. Lithium therefore comes out first as Li₃PO₄ (note 29) and E-501 then concentrates the Li-free filtrate (S-520) only to ~500 g/L sodium gluconate: ~9 t/h of water evaporated, returned as clean condensate (S-517 with the crystalliser condensates, ~10 t/h) to the fermenter medium and make-up on Sheets 1–3. MVR keeps this to roughly 25–35 kWh per tonne evaporated. Alternatives recorded and not taken: recycling the raffinate to R-301 (recycle_loop_check.py: it returns as sodium gluconate at pH 6.5 with no free acid) and bipolar-membrane electrodialysis to split the salt back into acid + base (the true closed loop; a Sheet 6 option).
  5. Li₃PO₄ at 60 °C with trisodium phosphate. Li₃PO₄ solubility is ~0.39 g/L (0.07 g/L as Li), two orders below Li₂CO₃, so the barren at ~1.9 g/L Li precipitates directly: 3 Li⁺ + PO₄³⁻ → Li₃PO₄. Na₃PO₄ at 10 % excess (~355 kg/h), 95 % yield assumed (residual ~0.1 g/L Li), 1 h residence, water wash on F-501; the excess phosphate leaves in the brine. Product 228 kg/h (1,821 t/yr) is a lower-value intermediate than battery-grade Li₂CO₃ — it is converted to Li₂CO₃ or LiOH by the buyer (H₃PO₄/Ca or electrochemical routes) — so the TEA's lithium line must be re-priced. Adds ~150 kg/h Na to the brine.
  6. The brine is the bleed. S-516 (~13.7 t/h at 60 °C, ~500 g/L sodium gluconate) carries essentially all the sodium (~630 kg/h from three NaOH additions plus the phosphate — set by pH targets in the gluconate buffer), ~4.9 t/h of gluconate, excess phosphate, organics, and residual Li/Co/Ni. Nothing salty goes back to the fermenter. It is the main COD (~4.4 t/h as O₂, gluconate ThOD ≈ 0.9 g/g) and salt duty on Sheet 6 — and the reason NaOH dosing is the number to minimise across Sheets 4–5. Technical-grade sodium gluconate (concrete admixture) is a possible outlet if phosphate and metals are controlled; food/pharma grades are not realistic from this stream.

Notes

  1. One liquid effluent, and it is dried, not discharged. The plant's only liquid bleed is the sodium/phosphate/gluconate stream: ~13.9 t/h combined (brine 13.7, purges 0.1, scrubber blowdown 0.1). Treating it biologically would be a 4.4 t/h COD (as O₂) duty — a wastewater plant bigger than the leach — and would throw away 4.9 t/h of gluconate that cost 4.8 t/h of glucose to make. Drying it instead gives ~6.1 t/h (49 kt/yr) of crude sodium gluconate (~90 % Na-gluconate, plus Na₂SO₄, excess phosphate, carbonate from the scrubber, and ppm Co/Ni/Li). Technical-grade outlets (concrete set-retarder, industrial chelating cleaners) exist at roughly $0.6–0.9/kg; food or pharma grade is not reachable from this stream without a separate purification plant. The TEA carries this salt at zero credit — metals recovery is the project's purpose, so the salt is an off-take obligation, not a revenue line, and the glucose behind it is an unoffset cost. Any off-take actually signed is upside on top of the metals case. Keshav's no-waste preference; the alternative — bipolar-membrane electrodialysis returning gluconic acid to R-301 and NaOH to Sheets 4–5 — is the true closed loop and is the option to study next.
  2. Water closes with a small fresh make-up. Condensate (crystallisers + E-501, ~10.4 t/h) supplies the S-104 make-up and most of the fermenter medium; the remainder of the medium (~5.8 t/h) and the F-301 wash (~1.6 t/h) are fresh water, less a 0.5 t/h condensate surplus — net ~6.9 t/h fresh. The D-601 exhaust carries ~7.8 t/h of water: condensing it would make the plant water-neutral, at the cost of a condenser and a slightly larger dryer. Water leaves in the product hydrates (~1.0 t/h), the three cakes (~0.7 t/h), the salt (0.3 t/h) and the dryer exhaust. The fermenter off-gas humidity is not counted.
  3. Solids leave as three cakes. Leach residue S-308 (~1.1 t/h wet; graphite, binder, unleached oxide and — on the Option A basis — the goethite/gibbsite that precipitates in R-301 at pH ~4.8), Fe/Al cake S-408 (~0.5 t/h wet), spent biomass S-210 (40 kg/h). None is hazardous by composition, but the residue carries fluoride from the electrolyte (not modelled) and should be leach-tested before a disposal route is fixed. Copper cement S-403 is a by-product, not a waste.
